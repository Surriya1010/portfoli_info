About

Robot learning has plenty of model architectures and compute. What it lacks is clean, well-labelled demonstration data, and that's the part I work on. On the SO-100 arm I wrote the accept/reject criteria for a multi-stage stacking task, graded more than 100 teleoperated episodes frame by frame against them, and traced the trained policy's failures back to either perception or trajectory errors.

Research · in progress

Which training-data errors break a VLA policy?

Nov 2026 – present

A controlled study of how realistic demonstration-data errors (timestamp offsets, task-label noise, operator-style corruption) change a vision-language-action policy. Each error type is injected on its own, at set strengths, into Franka/LIBERO data. The same smolVLA model is fine-tuned on each version, and the drop in task success is reported across seeds with 95% confidence intervals. The study also tests whether cheap checks can catch each error before training. The design, related work and a tested corruption and detector library are public. Experiments are in progress.

NVIDIA GR00T N1 closed-loop in Isaac Lab 3.0

Nov 2026

Runs NVIDIA's 2B-parameter GR00T N1 vision-language-action model closed-loop on GR1-T2 humanoid manipulation tasks in Isaac Lab 3.0. A small localhost bridge connects the simulator (Python 3.12) to the policy server (Python 3.10) and matches the reference image preprocessing. A hold-pose baseline separates simulator cost from model-inference cost.

Dataset curation & policy evaluation: visual imitation learning on SO-100

Sep – Dec 2025
  • Collected and reviewed 100+ teleoperated video episodes of a multi-stage object-stacking task, grading each frame by frame against written accept/reject criteria.
  • Wrote the criteria: what counts as completion, where sub-actions begin and end, and which near-misses (partial grasp, object drift, unstable approach) disqualify an episode.
  • Trained an ACT policy with smolVLA pre-training in PyTorch, then diagnosed its failures from rollout video, separating perception errors from trajectory errors.

Multi-robot coordination with Open-RMF

Jan – Apr 2025

A heterogeneous robot fleet coordinated through Open-RMF for collision-free operation in industrial environments, with VDA 5050 for fleet communication. It improved task effectiveness by about 30% in real-world testing. Micro-ROS runs on the low-level devices and Zenoh handles inter-robot communication.
