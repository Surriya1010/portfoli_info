About Me

My expertise spans multi-robot coordination, autonomous navigation, and visual imitation learning. I've worked on cutting-edge projects including a Harvard Medical School-funded autonomous wheelchair and multi-robot fleet management using Open RMF. Currently, I'm experimenting with Action Chunking Transformers (ACT) for robotic manipulation on the SO-100 arm.


I've gained international research experience at West Pomeranian University in Poland (3D magnetic imaging sensors) and IIT Madras Research Park (autonomous wheelchair localization). My work has been recognized with the Judge's Choice Award at eYIC National Finals 2023-24 at IIT Bombay.


My goal is to build embodied intelligence that bridges the gap between simulation and real-world deployment—robots that can truly sense, reason, and act autonomously.

Visual Imitation Learning on SO-100 Arm

Ongoing Research (Sep 2025 - Present)

Experimenting with visual imitation learning on the SO-100 robotic arm to bridge the sim-to-real gap. Leveraging pre-training on smol VLA (Vision-Language-Action model) for efficient learning. Implemented and fine-tuned Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) policy, achieving stable trajectory prediction for multi-stage stacking tasks. Conducted Domain Randomization experiments (varying lighting and camera poses) to analyze policy robustness against visual noise.

PyTorch Transformers (ACT) SO-100 Arm Vision-Language-Action Domain Randomization

Which Training-Data Errors Break a VLA Policy?

Research Write-up (Nov 2026 - Present)

A controlled study of how realistic demonstration-data errors (timestamp offsets, task-label noise, operator-style corruption) change a vision-language-action policy. One error type is injected at a time at set strengths into Franka/LIBERO data, the same smolVLA model is fine-tuned on each version, and the drop in task success is reported with seeds and 95% confidence intervals. It also tests whether cheap checks can detect each error before training. Design, related work and a tested corruption/detector library are public; experiments are in progress.

NVIDIA GR00T N1 Closed-Loop in Isaac Lab 3.0

Runs NVIDIA's 2B-parameter GR00T N1 vision-language-action model closed-loop on GR1-T2 humanoid manipulation tasks in Isaac Lab 3.0. A small localhost bridge connects the simulator (Python 3.12) to the policy server (Python 3.10), matching the reference image preprocessing, and a hold-pose baseline separates simulator cost from model inference cost.
